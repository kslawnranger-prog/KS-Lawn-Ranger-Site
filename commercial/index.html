What commercial service includes

The visit list is the same kind of work done at homes, scoped for a business property. That can be a weekly cut and edge along a storefront, bed weeding so planting areas stay defined, shrub trimming, a spring or fall cleanup with haul-away, or snow and ice work at entrances and other areas written into the quote.

A weekly mowing route means the property is on a repeating schedule through the growing season instead of being called in when the grass is already tall. Bi-weekly is an option when growth is slower. Mulching is standard. Bagging is quoted when you want clippings removed. Hard surfaces that are part of the visit get blown off.

Snow contracts for businesses

A seasonal snow contract is for a business that wants to be on the route before the first storm, rather than shopping for help after the snow is already down. The quote names the surfaces — walks, entrances, and any other areas you want cleared — and whether ice melt is included per visit or as part of the plan. Pre-treatment can be requested when the weather allows.

Bare pavement is not guaranteed. Refreeze, drifting, compaction, and traffic can make a surface slippery again after a visit. The job is to clear what is there at service time and open safe access. Details of timing are set in the quote. This site does not publish a response-time promise.

Insurance, invoices, and who to call

The company is fully insured, and proof is available on request — ask for it when you are comparing contractors for a commercial site. Invoices are available. Estimates and billing run through QuickBooks, and a QuickBooks payment link is one of the ways to pay, along with cash, check, and Venmo.
