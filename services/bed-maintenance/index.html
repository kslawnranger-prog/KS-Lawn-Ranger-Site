Bed weeding and maintenance.

Maintenance, as opposed to a new install

Bed maintenance is the upkeep: weeds pulled, grass creeping in from the lawn cut back, and the edge redefined. It is what keeps a mulch or rock bed from turning into the before photo in the gallery — tall weeds, lost edges, and soil showing through. If the bed needs a full layer of new mulch or rock, that is the install. If it needs to be held where it is, this is the service.

One cleanup visit is enough for some properties, especially in spring. Others, including storefront beds that are seen every day, want it on a schedule so the weeds never get a month’s head start. Say which you want. A commercial property can put bed maintenance next to a weekly mowing route and have both on the same account, with an invoice through QuickBooks if you need it.

What you should expect

Weeds that are pulled are removed, not left wilted on the mulch, when haul-away or cleanup is part of the quote. Edges are cut so the mower has a line to follow on the next visit. We do not promise a bed will never grow another weed. Seed blows in. The point of maintenance is that someone is scheduled to deal with it.

If shrubs in the same bed are overgrown, add trimming to the request. Doing the weeds and leaving a hedge on the walk is an unfinished job, and it is easy to price together. Spring and early summer are when beds get away from people. Fall is the other time, after leaves are out and before winter covers what was missed.
